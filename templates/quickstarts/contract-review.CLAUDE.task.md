# CLAUDE.md — contract review: [deal/vendor batch]

Task-specific guidance. Shared conventions live in the root `CLAUDE.md`. Every judgment
here is a discrete, named, evidenced claim: a risk flag cites the clause verbatim, with
section number.

## What this task is

Review each inbound contract against our playbook before signature. A unit of work = one
contract (the draft, its redlines, the term sheet, prior correspondence).

## Regime — what counts toward the outcome

The recommendation (sign / negotiate / escalate) is settled by the contract text against
the playbook. Business-relationship context is recorded as context, never as a reason to
pass a non-conforming clause without an explicit, named waiver.

## Criteria

| Component | Bar | Judged from |
|---|---|---|
| Liability cap | ≥ [floor] per playbook §[n] | contract §[liability] |
| Termination | for-convenience with ≤ [days] notice | contract §[term] |
| Data terms | meets [policy] | contract §[data], DPA |
| Deviations | every playbook deviation named + waived or negotiated | full text vs playbook |

## Sources of truth — verify every claim here, not from memory

- **The contract draft [path/version]** — authoritative for what it says. Quote clauses
  verbatim with section numbers; paraphrase-in-quotes is a blocker here more than
  anywhere.
- **The playbook [path/version]** — authoritative for our positions.
- **Authoritative *for the counterparty* (never a deviation for following it):** the term
  sheet and any positions we offered in writing [paths]. If the draft matches what we
  offered, that's our issue to renegotiate, not their non-conformance.

## Per-task gotchas

- Cross-references inside the contract are claims: "as defined in §2.1" is verified
  against §2.1, every time — misdirected cross-references are a classic drafting defect.
- A "standard clause" claim verifies against the playbook, not familiarity.
